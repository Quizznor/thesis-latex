\section{Monte Carlo Datasets}

Confirmed photon \CRs, detected e.g.~by the \LHAASO have reconstructed primary 
energies of $<\SI{10}{\peta\eV}$ 
\cite{lhaasocollaborationUltrahighenergyRayBubble2024}, less than 
\SI{10}{\percent} of the lower bound on the energy considered in this work. No 
photon candidates have ever been measured in the search space studied here.

With this lack of experimental evidence we must base our analysis on simulations
of \EASs. The following sections detail how these simulations are performed, and
present the datasets of simulated air showers used throughout 
\cref{chap:chapter-4}.

The code used in generating the simulation datasets are available at 
Ref.~\cite{AugerobservatorySandBoxesPaulfilip2026}.

\subsection{Air Shower Simulation}

\texttt{CORSIKA v7.8010} \cite{engelNextGenerationCORSIKA2018} is used to 
simulate air showers for both photon and proton primaries. In either case, 
primary particles with a true energy $E_\mathrm{MC}$ are injected with a heading
$(\phi_\mathrm{MC},\,\theta_\mathrm{MC})$ into the upper atmosphere, and the 
resulting particle cascade is simulated using \texttt{EPOS\_LHC} 
(Ref.~\cref{sssec:hadronic-interaction-models}, \cite{pierogEPOSLHCTest2015}), 
with \texttt{FLUKA  2011.2c} \cite{theflukacollaborationFLUKACodeOverview2024} 
handling particle interactions below a center-of-mass energy of 
\SI{80}{\giga\eV}.

\subsubsection{Particle Distribution}

Particles are injected at an atmospheric depth of 
\SI{0}{\gram\per\centi\meter\squared}, with the parametrized air density profile
emulating typically measured atmospheric conditions in Malargüe in September, as
described in further detail in 
Ref.~\cite{abreuDescriptionAtmosphericConditions2012}. 

The heading of the primary particle is randomized with the distribution of 
$(\phi_\mathrm{MC},\,\theta_\mathrm{MC})$ mimicking a uniform flux over the sky 
as observed by a flat detector. Specifically,
$\phi_\mathrm{MC}\sim\mathcal{U}(\SI{-180}{\degree},\,\SI{180}{\degree})$, and 
$\sin(2\,\theta_\mathrm{MC})\sim\mathcal{U}(0,\,0.766)$. The upper bound on the 
latter parameter corresponds to a maximum simulated zenith angle of 
$\SI{65}{\degree}$. The distribution of simulated primary zenith angles is shown
in \cref{fig:zenith-mc-distribution}.

The primary energy of injected particles is 
$\log_{10}\left(E_\mathrm{MC}\,/\,\mathrm{eV}\right)\in[16.5, 19.0]$, covering a
broad range from full inefficiency,\footnote{Meaning no \EAS below this energy 
is ever observed in the \SD-750.} to an upper energy where the size of the array
becomes a bottleneck in detecting the faint \CR fluxes. For an individual 
\texttt{CORSIKA} run the energy range of the particle is fixed to one tenth of a
decade in logarithmic energy. Within this bin, the energy is randomized such 
that $E^\mathrm{bin}_\mathrm{MC}\sim E$, meaning that higher values are picked 
more likely than lower values. In order to obtain sufficient sample sizes across
all energy bins lower energy showers are simulated more frequently than higher 
energy showers. Both considerations influence the distribution of simulated 
primary energies, as shown in \cref{fig:energy-mc-distribution}.

In order to be computationally efficient the simulation framework employs 
\emph{thinning} \cite{saffiAnalysisSimulationReconstruction2020}, which assigns
weights to individual particles in the shower cascade. A particle with weight 
$W > 1$ consequently approximates $W$ copies of itself, reducing the number of 
tracked particles, and cutting down on processing time. The threshold for the 
thinning algorithm to group particles together is energy dependent. This 
threshold is set dynamically to an optimal value of $10^{-6}\,E_\mathrm{MC}$ 
\cite{billoirSamplingProcedureRegenerate2008}.

The simulation of the shower cascade halts at an atmospheric depth corresponding
to an altitude of $\SI{1400}{\meter}\,\mathrm{a.s.l.}$, the height of the \PAOs 
detectors. At this point, the properties of the remaining particles in the 
simulated shower front are handed to an \Offline simulation that generates (\SD)
detector signals from the particle distribution on ground. In order to further 
optimize computational usage, we repeat this process multiple times per shower, 
varying the position of the \SD detector relative to the \EAS every time. Each 
\texttt{CORSIKA} simulation is passed to \Offline at most $50$ times, or until 
the \EAS has been reconstructed successfully $n_\mathrm{success} = 10$ times. 

\subsubsection{Detector Signal}

For a single reconstruction run, the shower core, referring to the intersection 
between the shower axis (fixed by $\phi_\mathrm{MC},\,\theta_\mathrm{MC}$) and 
the detector plane is picked at random. An arbitrary \SD-750 station is chosen, 
and the shower core is picked as a random point from a unit rectangle centered 
around this station. In this fashion, the distribution of simulated shower cores
mimics the uniform\footnote{Non-uniformities are caused by detector 
inefficiencies, see e.g.~Ref.~\cref{sssec:full-efficiency-validation}} 
distribution of \EASs core distributions the surface of the Earth. 

Once the shower core is picked, the entire geometry of shower and detector is 
fixed. Particles (with weight $W$) that fall within a predefined volume around 
an \SD-station are \emph{dethinned} 
\cite{saffiAnalysisSimulationReconstruction2020}, and particle trajectories
through the \WCD, \SSD, and \UMD, as well as resulting material interactions are
simulated using \texttt{Geant4} \cite{allisonRecentDevelopmentsGeant42016, 
allisonGeant4DevelopmentsApplications2006, 
agostinelliGeant4aSimulationToolkit2003}. The trajectory of light created in the
scintillating material of the \SSD and \UMD, as well as in the Cherenkov cone of
relativistic muons in the \WCD is propagated to the different sensor components,
eventually resulting a simulated detector response to the virtual particle flux.

On top of this, electronic noise induced by the \DAQ readout chain, as well as 
low-energy atmospheric background muons are injected into the detector 
simulation to increase the degree of realism of the calculations.

\subsection{Event Reconstruction}

The simulated detector response from the \WCD, \SSD, and \UMD is reconstructed 
according to the standard reconstruction procedure defined in 
\cref{ssec:sd-reconstruction} and \cref{ssec:umd-reconstruction}. The module 
configuration settings used for the bulk of the analysis are the default values 
used in the \texttt{icrc2025-test7} release of \Offline 
\cite{Icrc2025test7Release2025}. Influences due to changes in the configuration 
files will be examined in \todo{hyperparameter optimization chapter}.

\subsection{Reconstruction Efficiency}

An important characteristic in the subsequent analysis is the reconstruction 
efficiency of the \SD(-750) array. Together with the distribution of a priori 
present \CR particles (i.e. the \CR spectrum), it determines the energy-, and 
zenith distribution of \CR observations. In particular, 
$P_\mathrm{rec}(E_\mathrm{MC}, \theta_\mathrm{MC})$\footnote{We assume 
$P_\mathrm{rec}(E_\mathrm{MC}, \theta_\mathrm{MC}, \phi_\mathrm{MC}) = 
P_\mathrm{rec}(E_\mathrm{MC}, \theta_\mathrm{MC})\,\,\,\forall\,\phi_\mathrm{MC}
\in [-180\,^\circ,\,180\,^\circ]$} gives the probability that a \CR event with 
true energy $E_\mathrm{MC}$ and inclination $\theta_\mathrm{MC}$ is successfully
reconstructed. This is to say that the \texttt{LDFFinderKG} 
(Ref.~\cref{sssec:reconstruction-ldf-finder}) runs without emitting errors, and 
the event can be assigned a reconstructed energy $E_\mathrm{rec}$. 

Importantly, this probability differs from the often quoted array trigger 
efficiency $P_\mathrm{T3}$ 
(e.g.~\cite[Fig.~7]{abrahamTriggerApertureSurface2010}). The detection of an 
event does not guarantee its successful reconstruction, which the problem at 
hand, the identification of exotic events in reconstructed data, relies on. 
$P_\mathrm{rec}$ is thus the appropriate choice for normalizing different 
detector characteristics in this analysis.

We employ a simulation-driven parametrization of $P_\mathrm{rec}$ from 
Ref.~\cite{schulzMeasurementEnergySpectrum}. Namely,

\begin{align}
\label{eq:rec-efficiency-parametrization}
P_\mathrm{rec}(E_\mathrm{MC},\,\theta_\mathrm{MC};\,\alpha(\theta_\mathrm{MC}), \beta) &= \frac{1}{2}\left( 1+\erf(\frac{\log_{10}(E_\mathrm{MC}\,/\,\mathrm{eV}) - \alpha(\theta_\mathrm{MC})}{b}) \right), \\
\label{eq:efficiency-theta-dependence}
\alpha(\theta_\mathrm{MC};\,a_0,\,a_1,\,a_2,\,a_3) &= a_0 + a_1\,x + a_2\,x^2 + a_3\,x^3,\qquad x = \cos^2\,\theta_\mathrm{MC}\,.
\end{align}

$a_0$ roughly marks the energy at which the reconstruction algorithm becomes
efficient, with $P(a_0,\,\frac{\pi}{2}) = 0.5$. The other shape parameters $a_i$
represent higher order correction terms to the zenith dependence of the energy
scale set by $a_0$.

All parameters are determined from an unbinned maximum likelihood optimization 
tailoring \cref{eq:rec-efficiency-parametrization} to the reconstruction 
efficiencies calculated during the simulation procedure. These follow for a 
single simulation with energy $E_\mathrm{MC}$ and zenith $\theta_\mathrm{MC}$ by
building the ratio of number of successful reconstructions, 
$n_\mathrm{success}$, versus number of total attempted reconstructions. The 
results of the optimization, both for $p$ and $\gamma$ primaries is displayed in
\cref{tab:efficiency-fit-parameters}. The visual correspondence between the 
simulated reconstruction efficiency and the parametrized reconstruction 
efficiency for both primaries is shown in \cref{fig:efficiency-fits}.

Several things are noteworthy. First, it is $a_0^\gamma > a_0^p$, meaning that 
the reconstruction of proton primaries becomes possible at lower energies than 
that of photon primaries. Moreover, $|a_i^\gamma| > |a_i^p|$, implying that 
there exists a stronger zenith dependence of the reconstruction efficiency for 
photons.

This confirms prior expectations informed by air shower physics theory. The 
higher muon content of $p$ initiated \EASs results in a larger detector signal 
on ground, resulting in a higher chance for an event trigger and augmenting the 
probability for a successful event reconstruction. On the other hand, the 
non-muonic component of \EASs is more heavily attenuated while traversing the 
atmosphere. This disproportionately affects \EM-dominated photon showers, 
driving up the primary energy necessary to raise an event trigger as the 
inclination of the primary particle increases. Both effects can be seen in 
\cref{fig:efficiency-fits}.

\subsubsection{Validation of Full Efficiency Transition}
\label{sssec:full-efficiency-validation}

In order to verify the validity of this simulation-inferred reconstruction 
efficiency, we compare the proton reconstruction efficiency to estimations 
stemming from measured data.

Assuming an isotropic flux of \CR primaries, the inclination of the primary 
particle is distributed such that 
$\sin^2\,\theta_\mathrm{MC} \sim\mathcal{U}(0, \sin^2\,\theta_\mathrm{max})$.
If a \CR observatory detects all events above an energy $E_\mathrm{MC}$ and 
inclination $\theta_\mathrm{MC}$, the distribution of reconstructed arrival
directions should mirror this dispersion. Fewer observations, especially at high
inclinations are thus indicative of a reconstruction inefficiency.

The above logic can be formalized with an Anderson-Darling test
\cite{andersonAsymptoticTheoryCertain1952}, which we use to calculate the 
probability of observing a given zenithal distribution assuming the null 
hypothesis ($H_0$) of an isotropic flux.

To conduct this test, we take observations $O_i$ measured during Phase-I of the
\PAO (Ref.~\cref{sec:phase1-event-level-observables}). For a set of events 
$Y = \{ O\,|\,E_O > E_\mathrm{cut}\,\wedge\,\theta_O < \theta_\mathrm{cut}\}$ 
above energy $E_\mathrm{cut}$ and below an inclination $\theta_\mathrm{cut}$,
and ordered by ascending $\theta_\mathrm{rec}$, we calculate the 
Anderson-Darling test statistic

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:anderson-darling-test-statistic}
A^2 = -|Y| - \sum\limits_{i=1}^{|Y|} \frac{2i-1}{|Y|} \left(\log(F(O_i)) - \log(1 - F(O_{|Y|+1-i}))\right),
\end{equation}

where $F(O_i)$ gives the \CDF of observation $O_i$ in $Y$. Since we test for 
uniformity in $\sin^2\,\theta_\mathrm{rec}$, the sum in 
\cref{eq:anderson-darling-test-statistic} simplifies to

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:anderson-darling-sum}
S = \sum\limits_{i=1}^{|Y|} \frac{2i-1}{|Y|} \left[\log\frac{\sin^2\,\theta_{\mathrm{rec},\,i}}{\sin^2\,\theta_\mathrm{cut}} - \log\left(1-\frac{\sin^2\,\theta_{\mathrm{rec},\,|Y|+1-i}}{\sin^2\,\theta_\mathrm{cut}}\right)\right].
\end{equation}

After calculating $A^2$ for a given $E_\mathrm{cut}$ and $\theta_\mathrm{cut}$,
we build a p-value for $Y\subset H_0$ by comparison to $n=10,000$ bootstrapped 
datasets $N_i$ randomly drawn from $H_0$ with $|N| = |Y|$. We accept the null 
hypothesis if $p = |\{N_i\,|\,A_{N_i}^2 > A_Y^2\}|\,/\,n  > 0.005$.

The distribution of calculated p-values for a set of adaptively sampled 
\cite{nijholtPythonadaptiveAdaptiveVersion2026} observation thresholds 
$(E_\mathrm{cut},\,\theta_\mathrm{cut})$ is shown in 
\cref{fig:full-efficiency-validation}.

\subsection{Energy Assignment}

The previous section hints towards an important caveat in the analysis. The true
energy of observed \CRs is unknown, and can only be approximately reconstructed 
from a detector signal. The energy reconstruction presented in 
\cref{sssec:sd-reconstruction-energy-estimator} is tuned such that the 
reconstructed \SD energy matches the reconstructed \FD energy for all detected
hybrid events. Relying on the fact that \EASs have a higher muon fraction with 
increasing mass of the primary particle, this leads to systematic 
underestimation of the energy of $p$ and $\gamma$ events.

There are thus a set of energy scales that stand in disagreement with each 
other. The true \CR energy $E_\mathrm{MC}$ is different from the reconstructed
energy $E_\mathrm{rec}$. Moreover, each primary group (with the same 
$E_\mathrm{MC}$ and $\theta_\mathrm{MC}$) has a different bias in reconstructed
energy. To ensure a fair comparison, we must thus build an unbiased energy 
estimator that approximates $E_\mathrm{MC}$ from reconstructed observables for 
the two considered primaries.

\subsubsection{Simulated Protons}
\label{sssec:proton-energy-scale}

We use the approach in Ref.~\cite{ellwangerAttenuationModelsExtensive2025}, 
connecting the true primary energy to the reconstructed shower size $S(450)$
using a physically motivated attenuation function.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:proton-energy-scale}
\mathrm{log}_{10}\left(E_\mathrm{MC}\,/\,\mathrm{eV}\right) \approx A + B\,\mathrm{log}_{10}\left(\frac{S(450)\,/\,\mathrm{VEM}}{f(\theta_\mathrm{rec};\,q_\mathrm{EM}, \gamma, \delta)}\right),
\end{equation}

where $f$ treats the effective \EM component of the particle cascade different 
from the (quasi-unattenuated) muonic component; namely

\begin{align}
\label{eq:physically-motivated-attenuation-function}
f(\theta_\mathrm{rec};\,q_\mathrm{EM}, \gamma, \delta) &= q_\mathrm{EM}\,f_\mathrm{EM}(\theta_\mathrm{rec};\,\gamma, \delta) + (1-q_\mathrm{EM}) f_\mu, \\ 
\label{eq:electromagnetic-attenuation-function}
f_\mathrm{EM} &= \exp\left(\gamma\,x + (\gamma - \delta)\,y\right), \\
f_\mu &= 1.
\end{align}

with the substitutions

\begin{align}
\label{eq:attenuation-function-substitution}
x &= \sec\,\theta_\mathrm{rec} - \sec\,\theta_\mathrm{ref}, \\
y &= \log\sec\,\theta_\mathrm{rec} - \log\sec\,\theta_\mathrm{ref}, 
\end{align}

are in use in \cref{eq:electromagnetic-attenuation-function}. The direct energy
calibration parameters $A$ and $B$, the effective \EM fraction $q_\mathrm{EM}$, as well as the attenuation function parameters $\gamma$ and $\delta$ are again 
determined via an unbinned likelihood optimization where the reference angle 
$\theta_\mathrm{ref} = \SI{35.84}{\degree}$ is chosen as the median angle of 
the $340,806$ reconstructed simulated proton showers to ensure an accurate 
representation. The results of the numerical optimization are listed in 
\cref{tab:energy-scale-parameters}.

The obtained energy scale $E^p_\mathrm{rec}$ approximates the true primary 
energy of proton showers from reconstructed values with high accuracy over the
entire range of simulated parameters. The largest mean bias are well below 
$\SI{10}{\percent}$ and recorded at low primary energies and high zenith angles,
as shown in \cref{fig:proton-scale-energy} and \cref{fig:proton-scale-zenith}.

\subsubsection{Simulated Photons}

We employ the same optimization as in \cref{sssec:proton-energy-scale}, 
estimating the fit parameters for the $318,465$ simulated photon showers with 
$\theta_\mathrm{ref} = \SI{33.97}{\degree}$. The found parameters are listed in 
\cref{tab:energy-scale-parameters}, whereas the correspondence between the 
reconstructed photon energy $E^\gamma_\mathrm{rec}$ and the true energy is shown
in \cref{fig:photon-scale-energy} and \cref{fig:photon-scale-zenith}.

The accuracy of this photon energy scale is, on average, comparable to the 
proton energy scale in \cref{sssec:proton-energy-scale}. Slightly larger biases
of $\approx\SI{10}{\percent}$ at lower true primary energies and higher 
inclinations are indicative of an imperfect approximation of the \EM attenuation
function \cref{eq:electromagnetic-attenuation-function}, which is poorly
constrained in both optimization procedures.

We note qualitatively that $B^p > B^\gamma$, implying that the reconstructed
shower size of photon-induced \EASs grows slower than those of proton-induced
\EASs. This confirms expectations that the muonic component of the particle 
cascade leaves a larger detector trace than the \EM component, which, given 
$q_\mathrm{EM}^\gamma > q_\mathrm{EM}^p$ must be bigger for photon showers
compared to proton showers.

With the optimized parameters in \cref{tab:energy-scale-parameters} we have 
obtained two frameworks with which we can accurately approximate the primary
energy of simulated proton and photon primaries. However, the primary particle 
type of an observed \EAS is unknown, we do not know whether the "correct" energy
to assign to an event is $E^p_\mathrm{rec}$ or 
$E^\gamma_\mathrm{rec}$.\footnote{Or the energy scale set by simulations of 
other \CR types.} This calls for the creation of a novel, unified energy scale
that treats all \CR observations equally, independent of their (unknown) primary
type. Of particular importance is a small bias of the reconstructed energy for
photon primaries, as it defines the accuracy of the flux limits set in 
\cref{chap:photon-search}.

We construct a unified energy scale $E^\mathrm{mix}_\mathrm{rec}$ by mixing both
photon and proton best-fit parameters in \cref{tab:energy-scale-parameters} into
\cref{eq:proton-energy-scale}. In particular, we choose $A^\gamma$ to ensure an
accurate correspondence of $E^\mathrm{mix}_\mathrm{rec}$ to $E_\mathrm{MC}$ for
photon-induced \EASs. In order to model the atmospheric attenuation of the 
dominating hadronic background we choose $B^p$, $q^p_\mathrm{EM}$, $\gamma^p$, 
and $\delta^p$ in order to fix the unified energy scale.

This choice of parameters leads to an almost unbiased approximation of the true
energy of photon primaries from the full efficiency threshold to 
$E_\mathrm{MC} \approx 10^{18}\,\mathrm{eV}$ for showers with 
$\theta_\mathrm{rec}\in[\SI{0}{\degree},\,\SI{44}{\degree}]$, as shown in
\cref{fig:unified-energy-scale-vertical}. At higher inclinations 
(Ref.~\cref{fig:unified-energy-scale-horizontal}), the imperfect approximation 
of the attenuation function becomes evident and $E^\mathrm{mix}_\mathrm{rec}$ 
becomes biased for photon-induced \EASs.

The energy of proton-induced \EASs is overestimated. Therefore, upon comparison 
of particle cascades with the the same energy $E^\mathrm{mix}_\mathrm{rec}$, 
lower energy proton showers (with smaller muonic component) are mixed with 
higher energy photon showers. This is crucial in ensuring the extrapolated 
background contaminations for a given photon candidate cut in 
\cref{sec:ldf-based-discrimination} will be pessimistic, enforcing our 
confidence in gathered results.

Importantly, the proton energy bias, especially with respect to the photon bias,

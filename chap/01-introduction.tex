\chapter*{Introduction}
\label{chap:introduction}

\setlength{\parindent}{20pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{2pt}

Roughly ten years ago, I, the author of this thesis, had just started my 
undergraduate studies of physics at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology. I was
trying to keep up with all the different lectures I attended. Sometimes the work
load would be overwhelming, and would go out on a walk at night. I would find a 
bench to sit down on, and look up into the night sky to calm my mind.

This all seems impossibly far back to me now. I invite you to think back to what
your life was like ten years ago. So many things have changed. Some have changed
for the better, and some have changed for the worse. In a less personal sense, 
the world has shifted drastically. Moving past the ten year mark, the story of 
humanities technological advance is nothing short of awe-inspiring. A person
born in the late 19th century grew up before the invention of airplanes. Some 
60 years later, the first human set foot on the moon. 60 years later still, the
processing capabilities in a commercial smartphone far outshine that of the 
Apollo 11 computers.

What would Magellan or Columbus say to the GPS navigation system? Would the 
wanderings of Odysseus have been a similar epos if he had access to a sextant? 
Despite all the cultural and technological differences of so many different 
destinies across continents and epochs there exists one commonality that makes 
us human.

Since the beginning of documented history we have looked to the stars. Whether
it be for spiritual guidance or earthly navigation, the twinkling image of 
faraway stars has inspired a sense of wonder in us. We are curious about the 
cosmos, and have developed tools to probe the world around us.

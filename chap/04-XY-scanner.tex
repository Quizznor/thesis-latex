\section{The XY-Scanner}
\label{sec:xy-scanner}

The XY-Scanner (Ref.\ \cref{sssec:xy-scanner}) is a novel end-to-end calibration
system that is used to estimate the \FD calibration constants with an accuracy 
of $\sim\SI{5}{\percent}$. The measurement system as well as underlying analysis
is presented in detail in \cite{schaferXYScannerVersatileMethod2023}. It follows
a summary of Chapters 4, 6, and 7 of the aforementioned work.

\subsection{Light Source Calibration}
\label{ssec:xy-absolute-calibration}

The spirit of the \FD calibration procedure relies on the \FD-camera being
exposed to a light source of known radiance. This allows to tie the signal
recorded in the pixel-\PMTs to an incident count of photons, which holds
information about the energy of an \EAS (Ref.\ 
\cref{sssec:fd-reconstruction-energy-estimator}).

Different facilities have been set up to measure the radiance of the 
XY lightsource, shown in \todo{figure} and hereafter referred to as \CLS, 
independently \cite{heibgesPhotometricCharacterizationLight2020, 
schaferXYScannerVersatileMethod2023, vaculaVyvojKalibracnihoSvetelneho2024}. 
While precise implementation details differ, all test banks rely on measuring a 
distance-dependent flux of photons on a \PD that is illuminated by the \CLS. The
normalization of the expected inverse square law is given, to first order, by 
the geometry of the setup, as well as the \CLS radiance, as expressed in 
\cref{eq:source-radiance}.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:source-radiance}
\Phi_\mathrm{PD}(r) = \left(\frac{\pi r_\mathrm{CLS} \, r_\mathrm{det}}{r}\right)^2\,L_\mathrm{CLS}.
\end{equation}

\todo{figure} shows the agreement between measurement setups when testing the 
same \CLS. $L_\mathrm{CLS}$ can, thanks in part to this cross calibration, be 
determined with an accuracy of $\lessapprox\SI{3}{\percent}$. For detailed 
information regarding the individual setups see Ref.\ \todo{abscalpaper}

\subsection{Telescope Calibration}

The precisely determined radiance of the \CLS is then used to connect 
calibration data of the \FD telescopes to a corresponding calibration constant.
For this purpose the XY lightsource is installed in front of the \FD aperture on
two motorized linear stages as shown in \todo{fig}. Over the course of a 
measurement the light source tracks a path consisting of $n_\mathrm{pos}>1500$ 
unique positions distributed uniformly on a hexagonal grid that covers the 
entire telescope frame. At each position the \CLS emits a flash of light 
directed at the \FD-camera. The detector response is recorded for each position.

This results in measurement data consisting of $n_\mathrm{pos}$ events, each
containing the \ADC-traces digitized at the time of the \CLS flash for all 440 
pixels of the \FD-camera. The calibration constant for a given pixel-\PMT is 
then calculated from the measurement information according to the following 
steps:

\begin{enumerate}
	\item The baseline (Ref.\ \cref{sssec:reconstruction-pixel-calibrator})
	is subtracted from all traces in all \PMTs for all events.
	\item A pulse finding algorithm 
	(Ref.~\cite[][Section 7.1.1]{schaferXYScannerVersatileMethod2023}) 
	determines the part of the trace that contains the signal from the \CLS.
\end{enumerate}

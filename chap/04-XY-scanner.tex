\chapter{A New Fluorescence Energy Scale}
\label{chap:xy-scanner}

The analysis in the subsequent chapters heavily relies on information measured 
by the \WCD, \SSD, and \UMD. With the inauguration of the latter two detectors 
ocurring roughly ten years after the last official measurement of \FD pixel 
calibration constants \todo{cite}, the cross-calibration of the reconstructed 
\UMD/\SSD energies (Ref.~\cref{sssec:sd-sd-reconstruction-energy-estimator}) 
depend on measurement data that are more than a decade old. While the pixel 
calibration constants are in principle updated using a nightly relative 
calibration (Ref.~\cref{sssec:relative-calibration}) it is crucial to verify
the integrity of the relative tracking and the employed energy calibration.

The XY-Scanner is a novel end-to-end calibration system that is used to estimate
the \FD pixel calibration constants with an accuracy of \todo{uncertainty}. It 
is thus well suited to check the validity of the currently employed \FD 
calibration, and the \FD energy scale by extension. The measurement system as 
well as underlying analysis is presented in detail in 
\cite{schaferXYScannerVersatileMethod2023}, and summarized in 
\cref{sec:xy-scanner}.

\cref{sec:quality-assurance} details extensive quality checks that have been
developed in this work, and that are used to assure the reproducibility of XY 
measurements and establish the trustworthiness of the gathered data.

A comparison of XY measurement data to current pixel calibration constants can
be found in \cref{sec:telescope-aging}, where possible reasons for discrepancies
and an updated relative calibration tracking algorithm are given.

Finally, an updated Fluorescence energy scale is constructed in 
\cref{sec:fd-energy-scale}.

\subsection{Light Source Calibration}
\label{ssec:xy-absolute-calibration}

The spirit of the \FD calibration procedure relies on the \FD-camera being
exposed to a light source of known radiance. This allows to tie the signal
recorded in the pixel-\PMTs to an incident count of photons, which holds
information about the energy of an \EAS (Ref.\ 
\cref{sssec:fd-reconstruction-energy-estimator}).

Different facilities have been set up to measure the radiance of the 
XY lightsource, shown in \todo{figure} and hereafter referred to as \CLS, 
independently \cite{heibgesPhotometricCharacterizationLight2020, 
schaferXYScannerVersatileMethod2023, vaculaVyvojKalibracnihoSvetelneho2024}. 
While precise implementation details differ, all test banks rely on measuring a 
distance-dependent flux of photons on a \PD that is illuminated by the \CLS. The
normalization of the expected inverse square law is given, to first order, by 
the geometry of the setup, as well as the \CLS radiance, as expressed in 
\cref{eq:source-radiance}.

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:source-radiance}
\Phi_\mathrm{PD}(r) = \left(\frac{\pi r_\mathrm{CLS} \, r_\mathrm{det}}{r}\right)^2\,L_\mathrm{CLS}.
\end{equation}

\todo{figure} shows the agreement between measurement setups when testing the 
same \CLS. $L_\mathrm{CLS}$ can, thanks in part to this cross calibration, be 
determined with an accuracy of $\lessapprox\SI{3}{\percent}$. For detailed 
information regarding the individual setups see Ref.\ \todo{abscalpaper}

\subsection{Telescope Calibration}

The precisely determined radiance of the \CLS is then used to connect 
calibration data of the \FD telescopes to a corresponding calibration constant.
For this purpose the XY lightsource is installed in front of the \FD aperture on
two motorized linear stages, as shown in \todo{fig}. Over the course of a 
measurement the light source tracks a path consisting of $n_\mathrm{pos}>1500$ 
unique positions distributed uniformly on a hexagonal grid that covers the 
entire telescope frame. At each position the \CLS emits a flash of light 
directed at the \FD-camera. The detector response is recorded for each position.

The measurement process takes on average \SI{40}{\minute} without interruptions.
To check the stability of the \DAQ readout chain, two Calibration A measurements
with an open \FD shutter are performed immediately before and after the XY run.

Afterwards, the measurement data to be analyzed consists of $n_\mathrm{pos}$ 
($\mathcal{O}(1700)$) events, each containing the \ADC-traces digitized at the 
time of the \CLS flash for all 440 pixels of the \FD-camera. The calibration 
constant for a given pixel-\PMT is then calculated from the measurement 
information according to the following steps:

\begin{enumerate}
	\item The baseline (Ref.~\cref{sssec:reconstruction-pixel-calibrator})
	is subtracted from all traces in all \PMTs for all events.
	\item A pulse finding algorithm 
	(Ref.~\cite[][Section 7.1.1]{schaferXYScannerVersatileMethod2023}) 
	determines the part of the trace that contains the light pulse from the 
	calibration light source.
	\item The integral of the pulse is calculated, this value is summed for 
	all events in each pixel to yield a total signal per pixel in units of 
	\ADC.
	\item The total number of photons received by each pixel-\PMT is 
	calculated via the known source radiance. The non-uniform camera 
	geometry and reflectivity are taken into account via simulation based 
	correction factors for each pixel.
	\item The total signal in every \PMT is divided by the total count of 
	incident photons to calculate the pixel calibration constant in units of
	$\ADC/\gamma$.
\end{enumerate}
